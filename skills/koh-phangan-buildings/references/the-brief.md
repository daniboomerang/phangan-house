# What Daniel wants

Written down so it stops being rediscovered. When a design decision conflicts
with something here, say so rather than quietly dropping it.

## The one organising idea

**The house looks at the sunset.** Everything else bends to that. The sunset arc
is 246° in December, 270° at equinox, 294° in June. The road bears 29.75°, so a
base parallel to the road faces 299.75° — close enough.

**The base — the longest part — runs parallel to the road.** From the kitchen
and from the platform you see the sunset. Bedrooms in the arm face it too.

## Non-negotiable

- **High ceilings.** The single most important quality to him. Never trade
  ceiling height away without saying out loud what it costs.
- **Kitchen, dining and living as one big open room**, open to the roof.
- **The platform** — a mezzanine with the sofas and TV, not a separate floor.
  From it you look **down into the kitchen** and **out at the sunset**.
- **Master bedroom** with a dressing area and a **3 × 3 bath**.
- **Bedrooms at least 4 × 5.**
- **Storage at least 3 × 2, inside the house.**
- **A toilet on the ground floor.**
- **A tall entrance**, open to the roof.

## Strong preferences

- Round porthole windows on the outer faces rather than rectangles. One large
  one, about 2.1 m, on the platform facing the road.
- Sliding glass, full width, from the living room to the courtyard or terrace.
- Full glass from the master bedroom to its terrace.
- Rooms flat inside. Steps between levels are acceptable and land on
  thresholds; steps inside a room are not.
- **No concrete crossing the yard.** Paving hugs the walls; the middle stays
  planted.
- Paths run the full inside face of the building, and every door opens onto flat
  ground at its own floor level.

## The other buildings

- **Shala**, 6 × 6, on posts, no earthworks. Deck about 90.55 m, posts 0.35 to
  3.09 m. A meditation platform, low and wide, looking at the sunset.
- **Studio house** for music, on the lower plot, floating on posts.
- **Pool** at the rock face, 2 to 3 m below the courtyard, the rock forming its
  inland edge.

## How he works

- Directive. He knows what he wants and expects it executed, not reinterpreted.
- He spots geometry errors fast — mirrored plans, floating volumes, doors buried
  in slopes. **Check orientation against the 2D plans before showing 3D.**
- He wants drawings, not documents. A plan means walls, doors, swings, windows,
  furniture, dimensions — not a table with a diagram attached.
- When something does not fit, he wants the number and the trade, not a
  substitute design. **Never replace his design with a generic one because a
  constraint is hard.**

  KC must extend the survey northeast before corners are agreed.
- Whether the C-house survives as one building or splits into two detached ones.
  Depends entirely on the building-count question in `thai-hillside-law`.
- Ana, his architect and best friend, will produce the professional design. The
  role here is to give her a verified envelope and check what she sends back.
