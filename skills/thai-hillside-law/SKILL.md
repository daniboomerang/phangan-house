# Thai hillside building law

Covers the environmental protection notification for Ko Samui, Ko Pha-ngan and
Ko Tao, and the Building Control Act provisions it relies on.

**Read `references/notification.md` before answering anything about limits.**
It has the clauses. `references/height-measurement.md` and
`references/building-types.md` cover the two questions that decide most designs.

## The rules that bite, in order

| | |
|---|---|
| Zone 3(a) | 80–140 m above mean sea level, **both islands** |
| Building type | **detached only** — see `building-types.md`, this is a type not a count |
| Height | **6 m** — measurement point is the whole argument, see `height-measurement.md` |
| Ground coverage | **90 m² per building** |
| Open space | 50% under the elevation clause; **70% permeable** if slope is 35–50% |
| Green area | ≥50% of the open space, local species |
| Cut or fill | **1.00 m from original ground**, foundations and basements excepted |
| Graded slope | not steeper than 3:1 |
| Slope over 50% | **no building at all** |
| Roof | pitched, Thai / tropical / vernacular, ≥80% of building area, natural colours |
| Eligibility | land owned or possessed **before the 2014 notification** |

## The four traps

1. **Coverage is the shadow, not the house.** 90 m² caps the ground the
   building touches. Stack it and 90 m² becomes 180 m² of floor plus a roof
   loft. Never tell someone their house must be 90 m².
2. **Two clauses can both apply.** Elevation gives 50% open space; slope 35–50%
   gives 70% permeable. The stricter governs. Check slope as well as height.
3. **Detached means touching nothing.** A shared beam — even underground — makes
   two buildings semi-detached, which Zone 3(a) does not permit at all.
4. **Big villas are usually grandfathered or pavilions.** Seeing one is not
   evidence a rule doesn't exist.

## What cannot be answered from documents

Listed in `references/open-questions.md`. The two expensive ones are how the 6 m
is measured on a slope, and whether several detached buildings are permitted on
one plot. Both are OrBorTor questions. Give the user the precise wording to ask
rather than a guess.
