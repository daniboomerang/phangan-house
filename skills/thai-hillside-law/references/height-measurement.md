# How the 6 m is measured

The notification says "ความสูงไม่เกิน 6 เมตร" and does not define measurement.
So the Building Control Act ministerial regulations govern.

## The Act's definition

> Building height is measured from **the ground level at which it is
> constructed** (ระดับพื้นดินที่ก่อสร้าง) to the roof deck. For buildings with a
> **gable or hip roof** (ทรงจั่วหรือปั้นหยา), it is measured from that ground
> level to **the top of the wall of the highest storey** (ยอดผนังของชั้นสูงสุด).

## Why this matters more than anything else in the design

**The notification requires a pitched roof.** So the gable/hip rule should
apply, and **the entire roof volume sits above the measured height**. A loft in
the roof space is free area.

On a 4.4 m span at 26°, the ridge is about 1.07 m above the wall head. On a
wider span it is much more.

## The two readings on sloping ground

"ระดับพื้นดินที่ก่อสร้าง" is ambiguous on a slope, and the gap is expensive.

| Reading | Effect on a 3.9 m fall under the footprint |
|---|---|
| Natural ground **at each point** | envelope follows the slope; the low end governs; roughly 2.7 m of ceiling on a flat floor |
| **Mean** ground under the building | flat envelope; roughly 4.7 m |
| The **constructed** ground level | most generous; the floor is the datum |

**Worth about 2 m of ceiling height. Unresolved.** Do not present any one of
these as settled.

## What to do about it in practice

Design so the answer does not matter:

- **Step the floor with the hill.** Posts spend the height budget, benches give
  it back. A flat floor across 14 m of a 32% slope needs 2.75 m posts at the low
  end and leaves 2.72 m of ceiling. Three benches leave 4.6–4.9 m.
- Report both readings with the gap in metres, and let the user take the
  question to the OrBorTor with a number attached.

## Precise wording to ask

> ความสูง 6 เมตร ตามประกาศฯ วัดจากระดับพื้นดินเดิม ณ จุดใดจุดหนึ่ง
> หรือวัดจากระดับพื้นดินเฉลี่ยใต้อาคาร และกรณีหลังคาทรงจั่ว
> วัดถึงยอดผนังชั้นสูงสุดหรือถึงสันหลังคา
