# บ้านเดี่ยว, บ้านแฝด, ทาวน์เฮ้าส์

Zone 3(a) permits **บ้านเดี่ยวหรืออาคารเดี่ยว**. Getting this wrong costs a
whole building.

## They are types, not counts

Under the Building Control Act 2522 these are residential **categories**,
distinguished by shared structure:

| Term | English | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **บ้านเดี่ยว** | detached house | shares no wall or structure with anything |
| **บ้านแฝด** | semi-detached | two houses built as a pair sharing one wall **or any other connected part** |
| **ทาวน์เฮ้าส์ / บ้านแถว** | townhouse, row house | joined in a row along a single line |

## The reading that follows

"บ้านเดี่ยวหรืออาคารเดี่ยว" most likely restricts the **type** — no townhouses,
no semi-detached, no apartment blocks — rather than the **number**.

Three things support this:

1. The coverage cap is written **ต่อหลัง, per building**. That phrasing is
   meaningless if only one building is allowed.
2. Both islands are covered in multi-pavilion villas.
3. Nothing in clause 6 states a numeric limit on buildings.

**This is an interpretation, not a confirmed answer.** Flag it as such.

## The trap

Thai practice classifies as **บ้านแฝด** even houses joined only by an
**underground beam**, with no above-ground connection at all. Some are joined
only at a kitchen or a service room.

So two structures intended to be separate must share **nothing**: no wall, no
slab, no beam, no continuous roof, no covered link. Own foundations, own roof,
air between them.

Get that wrong and they become semi-detached, which Zone 3(a) does not permit at
all — a worse outcome than one large building.

## Precise wording to ask

> คำว่า "บ้านเดี่ยวหรืออาคารเดี่ยว" ในข้อ ๖ (๒) (ง)
> จำกัดจำนวนอาคารในแปลงที่ดินหรือจำกัดเฉพาะประเภทอาคาร
> และต้องเว้นระยะห่างเท่าใดจึงจะถือว่าเป็นอาคารเดี่ยวแยกจากกัน ไม่ใช่บ้านแฝด
