The yard is flat now — properly

Last version I laid flat slabs but left the terrain untouched underneath, so the natural slope kept poking through and walled off the door. The ground mesh itself is now cut to the bench levels across the whole footprint and courtyard, on a 133 × 81 grid so the cut edge is sharp.

All within the 1 m limit. About 18 m³ across the yard. The cut edge at the courtyard boundary becomes a low retaining bank — up to 0.93 m at the far end — which is where planting sits.

Paving against the walls only

A 1.2 m strip against every inner face — across the base band, down both arms at both levels, and across the open sunset end. Nothing crosses the yard. The middle is planted ground on two flat terraces.

Why the roofs step

They are not misaligned — each roof sits on its own height limit, and the limit follows the ground down the hill.

Down the hill the steps are 1.02 m and 1.11 m — deliberate, and the same size as the floor steps below them. Across the courtyard the two arms differ by only 0.18 and 0.32 m, which is what reads as “almost aligned but not quite”.

I tested levelling them. Bringing the right arm down to the left arm’s roof leaves 1.98 m over the bedroom. The offset is the price of two arms standing on different ground.
